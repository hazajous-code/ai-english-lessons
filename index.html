What Do You Tell a Chatbot?

Ads inside ChatGPT, conversations instead of keywords, and new rules for AI-made product images. Level: B1–B2 (intermediate to upper-intermediate) adults · 75 minutes.

By the end of the lesson, students will be able to:

  1. Read a 500-word news text about AI and advertising and identify main ideas and key details
  2. Learn and use 12 topic words (e.g., pilot, mislead, take effect)
  3. Use modal verbs to talk about rules: obligation (must, have to), prohibition (must not, may not), no obligation (don't have to) and permission (can)
  4. Agree on a set of company rules for AI-made images in a role-play

04 · Reading

What Do You Tell a Chatbot?

Adapted from AI Trend Search Vol.03 · about 540 words

1. A few words or a whole story?

In a search box, people type a few words, such as "running shoes." To a chatbot, they often tell a story: "I'm running my first 5K next month — what are some comfortable beginner shoes?" (This is a made-up example, not a real user's message.) This difference is starting to change online advertising.

2. Ads arrive in ChatGPT

On October 21, OpenAI announced that it had added 26 countries to ChatGPT ads, bringing the total to 89. A pilot has been running in Korea since June 19. OpenAI said that more than 100,000 advertisers have run campaigns in the little over eight months since ads launched.

Only adult users on the Free and Go plans see ads. There are no ads on higher paid plans, such as Plus, Pro and Enterprise. Ads are labeled "Sponsored" and appear separately from the answer. They don't appear in conversations about sensitive topics such as health, mental health and politics. According to OpenAI, advertisers don't see the content of conversations; they only get total numbers, such as views and clicks.

The big change is how ads are matched to conversations. Instead of keywords, advertisers write "context hints": sentences that describe what is useful, to whom, and when. In OpenAI's example, "running shoes" becomes "cushioned everyday running shoes for beginners training for their first 5K."

The business is growing fast. According to CNBC, the annualized revenue of ChatGPT's ad business grew from $100 million in March to $1 billion in August. But some numbers need care. The 100,000 figure counts every advertiser that has ever run a campaign. The Information reported about 20,000 active advertisers at the end of August. Early advertisers report a wide range of costs per click, from under $3 to $10–13.

3. Is that photo real?

On October 22, two new rules about AI-made images arrived on the same day. First, the handmade marketplace Etsy said every listing must include at least one unedited photo of the real item. Patterns and recipes may not be AI-generated. If a buyer reports that an item is "not as described" and the seller didn't follow the image rules, the seller may have to give a refund. Etsy hasn't said when the rule starts.

Second, the governor of the US state of Pennsylvania signed a law called SB 806. Ads that use AI-made images, video or audio to mislead consumers about a product must carry a label. It must say the ad "contains content modified or generated by AI." Companies can be fined up to $3,000. The law takes effect 60 days after signing, and it applies whether or not the advertiser meant to mislead.

Korea already has a similar rule. Since June 1, an AI-generated virtual person in an ad must be labeled a "virtual person."

None of these rules bans AI. They ban misleading customers. A simple test is: would a customer be surprised when the real item arrives?

4. What the editors think

The newsletter's editors argue that ad tools are open to every company equally. What makes the difference, they say, is knowing customers' situations: when, who and why they need a product, in the customers' own words. In their view, companies should be able to write down the situations customers describe to AI. This is the editors' opinion, not a proven fact.

Check your understanding

  2. True or false? Users on the Plus plan see ads in ChatGPT.
  3. True or false? More than 100,000 advertisers are running ads in ChatGPT right now.
  4. True or false? The Pennsylvania law applies only when an advertiser means to mislead.
  5. What do advertisers write instead of keywords, and what do these sentences describe?
  6. What must every Etsy listing include?
  7. What can happen to an Etsy seller who doesn't follow the image rules?
  8. What simple test does the text suggest for AI-made product images?
Answer key
  1. True — OpenAI added 26 countries on October 21, bringing the total to 89.
  2. False — only adult users on the Free and Go plans see ads; there are no ads on Plus, Pro or Enterprise.
  3. False — 100,000 counts every advertiser that has ever run a campaign; The Information reported about 20,000 active advertisers at the end of August.
  4. False — it applies whether or not the advertiser meant to mislead.
  5. "Context hints": sentences that describe what is useful, to whom, and when.
  6. At least one unedited photo of the real item.
  7. If a buyer reports the item is "not as described," the seller may have to give a refund.
  8. Would a customer be surprised when the real item arrives?

06 · Language focus

Modal verbs for rules: must, have to, must not, don't have to, can

When we talk about rules, laws and company policies, we use modal verbs (+ base verb, no to after must or can).

  • Obligation (it is necessary): must, have to / has to, need to
  • Prohibition (it is not allowed): must not (mustn't), may not, can't
  • No obligation (it is not necessary, but you can): don't have to / doesn't have to
  • Permission and possibility: can, may — and may have to (an obligation that is possible, not certain)

Be careful: must not and don't have to have very different meanings. You must not use AI = it is forbidden. You don't have to use AI = it's your choice.

For rules about things, we often use a passive modal: must + be + past participle (e.g., must be labeled).

From the text

  • Etsy said every listing must include at least one unedited photo of the real item. (obligation)
  • Patterns and recipes may not be AI-generated. (prohibition — here may not means "are not allowed to")
  • The seller may have to give a refund. (possible obligation)
  • Ads that use AI-made images … must carry a label. (obligation)
  • An AI-generated virtual person in an ad must be labeled a "virtual person." (passive modal)
  • Companies can be fined up to $3,000. (possibility)

Task 1

Choose the correct option, based on the reading.

  1. Every Etsy listing (must / doesn't have to) include at least one real photo of the item.
  2. Etsy sellers (can / may not) use AI to create images of patterns or recipes.
  3. Under the new rules, sellers (must not / don't have to) stop using AI completely.
  4. In Pennsylvania, ads that use AI to mislead consumers (must / don't have to) carry a label.
  5. A seller who doesn't follow the image rules (may have to / mustn't) give a refund.
  6. In Korea, an AI virtual person in an ad (has to / doesn't have to) be labeled.
Answer key
  1. must
  2. may not
  3. don't have to — none of the rules bans AI.
  4. must
  5. may have to
  6. has to

Task 2

A marketing team is writing its own rules. Rewrite each sentence using the word(s) in brackets.

  1. It is necessary to check every AI image before we publish it. (must)
  2. It is not allowed to show AI-made models as real customers. (must not)
  3. It isn't necessary to label ordinary photos of the real product. (don't have to)
  4. Team members are allowed to use AI to make first drafts of designs. (can)
Answer key

Suggested answers

  1. We must check every AI image before we publish it.
  2. We must not (mustn't) show AI-made models as real customers.
  3. We don't have to label ordinary photos of the real product.
  4. Team members can use AI to make first drafts of designs.

07 · Discussion

Talk about it

Choose three questions and discuss them in small groups.

  1. What kinds of questions do you ask chatbots? Would you mind seeing ads next to the answers?
  2. ChatGPT doesn't show ads in conversations about health, mental health or politics. Which other topics should be ad-free?
  3. Have you ever bought something online that looked different from the photo? What happened?
  4. Should every AI-made image in an ad have a label, or only the misleading ones? Why?
  5. The Pennsylvania law applies even when an advertiser didn't mean to mislead. Is that fair?
  6. The editors think knowing customers' situations matters more than ad tools. Do you agree? Why or why not?

08 · Role-play

Meeting: Our rules for AI-made product images

You work for a home goods company. It sells handmade items on its own website and on Etsy, and it runs online ads for customers in the US. To save time and money, the design team has started making product photos and model shots with AI. After the new Etsy rules and the Pennsylvania law, the company needs three written rules by Friday.

A — Creative lead You want to keep using AI images. They are faster and cheaper, and customers like them. Suggest rules that still let the team use AI.

B — Compliance manager You want strict rules. You worry about refunds, fines and losing customers' trust. Suggest, for example, a real photo for every product and a label on every AI image.

C — Head of marketing (chairs the meeting) Listen to both sides and ask questions. At the end, agree on three rules and read them out, using must, must not and don't have to.

Useful language

I think we must… · We don't have to… as long as… · We can…, but we mustn't… · What worries me is… · I'm not sure that's necessary. · Shall we agree that…? · So our three rules are…

09 · Writing

Your turn to write

Write an email (100–150 words) to your team announcing three new rules for using AI images or AI tools in your company's work. Use must, must not (or may not), don't have to and can at least once each, and give a reason for at least one rule.

10 · Teacher's notes

Tips for this lesson

  • Focus point: must not (forbidden) vs don't have to (not necessary). Task 1 item 3 tests this directly; use the text's line "None of these rules bans AI" to show why don't have to is correct. Also point out that may not in rules means "not allowed," not "perhaps not."
  • Pronunciation: mustn't /ˈmʌsnt/ (the first t is silent), have to /ˈhæftə/, has to /ˈhæstə/, mislead /mɪsˈliːd/ → misled /mɪsˈled/, annualized /ˈænjuəlaɪzd/.
  • Common error: adding to after must (*We must to check) and making must negative with don't (*You don't must). Correct forms: We must check; You mustn't / You don't have to.
  • Lower-level groups: pre-teach advertiser, campaign, marketplace and governor; read Part 3 together before pair reading; skip the revenue and cost-per-click details in Part 2.
  • Higher-level groups: contrast must (the speaker's own rule) with have to (an outside rule), and ask students to write two "context hints" for a product they know, following OpenAI's example in Part 2.
