When Anyone Can Copy Your Features

01 · Warm-up

Before you read

Discuss with a partner.

  1. Think of a product you love. Is it special because of what it does, or because of something else?
  2. Have you ever seen an ad or a picture and thought, "That was made by AI"? How did you feel?
  3. Would you let an app buy something for you without checking first?

03 · Reading

When Anyone Can Copy Your Features

Adapted from AI Trend Search Vol.01 · about 500 words

1. Copying software with AI

For decades, copying the features of a program without its source code took skilled people months or years. This is called reverse engineering. In October, a free tool called REA was released. It connects AI coding agents to reverse engineering tools, so the agents can try again and again without a human.

The results have spread quickly. One developer ran AI coding agents for about three months to rebuild a 2009 video game, Call of Duty: Modern Warfare 2. A developer used AI to build copies of seven Adobe apps and of Word, Excel and PowerPoint, although all of them are still in alpha and one reviewer called the Photoshop clone "completely unusable." On October 13, Xbox announced a new team to research AI agents and this kind of game decompilation itself.

2. Cheap attacks

Attackers are using AI too. According to the security firm Gambit, one attacker used three free AI tools to scan online stores and other websites. The average AI cost was about $25 per completed scan. At least 27 companies were breached, and 600,000 credit card records were stolen from two of them. The code that stole card details was hidden in places like the Google tags that marketing teams add to their websites. In Korea, several banks were breached by a hacker whose skill was described as "very low."

3. An agent that shops for you

On September 8, Meta launched Muse, an AI agent, in the United States. Muse can search for products, make bookings, send emails and pay on a customer's behalf. Meta says it always asks for the user's approval before it sends a message or buys something, and it has promised not to put ads inside Muse. That means brands cannot simply pay to be seen there.

4. The backlash against "AI slop"

Cheap AI content, often called "AI slop," is now causing problems for brands. Fanta's AI-generated Halloween ad was criticized online, with some people saying it cheapened the brand. Knockoffs of real books, apparently made with AI, appeared on Amazon. Meanwhile, Apple released a film of two sisters embroidering its famous rainbow logo by hand.

A study by Ipsos and Syracuse University, which tested 20 ads, predicted that human-made ads would perform better than AI-made ones. The newsletter's editors conclude that AI can copy features, but it cannot copy the work of understanding what customers really want. That is their opinion, and you may disagree.

04 · Comprehension

Check your understanding

  1. True or false? Before AI, reverse engineering software was quick and easy.
  2. True or false? The Adobe and Office copies are finished and ready for everyday use.
  3. True or false? $25 was the full cost of one successful break-in.
  4. True or false? Brands can pay to advertise inside Muse.
  5. Where was the card-stealing code hidden?
  6. What does Muse do before it buys something?
  7. Why did people criticize Fanta's Halloween ad?
  8. Is the Ipsos and Syracuse result based on real sales? How do you know?

05 · Language focus

The passive voice in news writing

We use the passive (be + past participle) when the action is more important than the person who did it, or when we don't know who did it.

Common news forms: present continuous passive (is/are being + p.p.) for changes happening now; past simple passive (was/were + p.p.) for finished events. Add by + agent only when it is useful.

From the text

  • Features are being copied faster than ever.
  • In October, a free tool called REA was released.
  • At least 27 companies were breached.
  • The code was hidden in Google tags.
  • Several banks were breached by a hacker whose skill was described as "very low."

Task 1

Rewrite these sentences in the passive. Leave out the agent if it isn't important.

  1. Someone stole 600,000 card records.
  2. Meta launched Muse on September 8.
  3. People are criticizing the ad online.
  4. Amazon removed the fake book the next day.
  5. Two sisters embroidered the logo by hand.
  6. Developers are using AI to copy old games.

Task 2

Active or passive? Choose the better form and explain why.

  1. (a) Someone took down the website within hours. / (b) The website was taken down within hours.
  2. (a) Xbox announced a new research team. / (b) A new research team was announced by Xbox.
  3. (a) Hackers hid the code in Google tags. / (b) The code was hidden in Google tags.

06 · Discussion

Talk about it

Choose three questions and discuss them in small groups.

  1. If a competitor could copy your product's features in a few weeks, what would make customers still choose you?
  2. Would you trust an AI agent to buy things for you? Where would you draw the line?
  3. Have you seen "AI slop"? Does it change how you feel about a brand?
  4. Should companies always disclose when they use AI to make ads or content? Why or why not?
  5. Is a hand-made product worth more to you? Give an example.
  6. Do you agree that understanding customers is something AI can't copy?

07 · Role-play

Team meeting: Should we use AI to make our next ad?

You work in the marketing team of a mid-sized snack brand. The next campaign is in two months, the budget is small, and an AI studio has offered to make the whole ad cheaply.

A — Marketing manager You want to use AI. It's fast and cheap, and the budget is tight. Suggest how to keep quality high.

B — Brand manager You are worried. You've seen the backlash against AI ads and think customers may feel the brand doesn't care.

C — Team leader (chairs the meeting) Listen to both, ask questions and decide. Also agree on one rule: when will the team disclose AI use?

Useful language

I'd suggest that… · My concern is that… · Have we thought about…? · That's a fair point, but… · How about a compromise? · Let's agree that…

08 · Writing

Your turn to write

Write a short team guideline (100–150 words) titled "How we use AI in our content." Include at least three rules and at least three passive sentences (for example, "All AI-made images are checked by a person before they are published.").
